# Sturum — Goals & Vision

This document describes **why Sturum exists**, **who it is for**, and **what success looks like**. It is the north star for product decisions, launches, and future features.

---

## Vision

**Sturum is the home base for students in the same academic department** — a place to find coursemates, share what matters for your program, and stay connected without the noise of the open internet.

We are not building another global social network. We are building a **department-scoped student community** that respects how university life actually works: your cohort, your level, your materials, your conversations.

---

## The Problem

Today, student life is spread across tools that were never designed for academic peers:

| Need | Typical workaround |
|------|-------------------|
| Finding coursemates in your department | WhatsApp invites, word of mouth, random DMs |
| Sharing past questions, handouts, notes | Telegram drives, Google Drive links, “DM me for the PDF” |
| Department news and discussion | Instagram, X, or overloaded group chats |
| One-to-one follow-up | WhatsApp |

These approaches work informally, but they are **fragmented**, **hard to search**, **easy to lose** when groups churn, and **not organized by department or academic level**.

Official university systems (LMS, portals) focus on courses and administration. They rarely solve **peer community** and **student-to-student resource sharing** in a way that feels natural to students.

---

## What Sturum Aims to Solve

Sturum brings the most common peer needs into **one application**, with a clear boundary: **your department**.

1. **Connection** — Discover and connect with students in the same department (not the entire university feed).
2. **Community feed** — Post updates, comment, like, and reshare — a lightweight board for your cohort.
3. **Real-time messaging** — Chat with people you are connected to, when WhatsApp groups are too noisy or too private.
4. **Study materials** — Upload and browse department-scoped resources (past questions, handouts, books, images).
5. **Identity** — Profiles tied to department and academic level so context is always clear.

The goal is to reduce friction between “I need something from my coursemates” and “I found it or found them.”

---

## Who It Is For

**Primary audience:** University students, especially those in specialized or tight-knit programs (e.g. marine, maritime, and related fields — Sturum’s original focus).

**Ideal early adopters:**

- A single department or faculty piloting the platform
- Students who already share materials informally and want a searchable, persistent place for them
- Course reps, class groups, and peer study communities looking for a dedicated hub

**Not the primary audience (for now):**

- General public social networking
- Staff or official university administration workflows
- Cross-university or cross-department open discovery

---

## Product Principles

These principles guide what we build and what we decline.

1. **Department-first** — Content, discovery, materials, and connections respect department boundaries. Trust comes from shared academic context.
2. **Student-built experience** — Flows match student mental models: level, department, materials types, coursemate language.
3. **Simple over sprawling** — Prefer a focused feature set that works together over copying every feature from LinkedIn or WhatsApp.
4. **Real utility** — A new signup should quickly see value: people, posts, or materials in *their* department.
5. **Ship and learn** — Launch in real communities, measure usage, iterate from feedback.

---

## Core Goals (What We Optimize For)

| Goal | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Belonging** | Students feel they are among coursemates, not strangers on the internet |
| **Discoverability** | Easier to find people and materials relevant to their program |
| **Persistence** | Posts and uploads remain findable after chat history scrolls away |
| **Safety of scope** | Department limits reduce irrelevant or random interactions |
| **Adoption in one wedge** | Win one department cohort before trying to serve an entire campus |

---

## Success Metrics (Pilot Phase)

Sturum is succeeding in a pilot when:

- Students in **one target department** sign up and return after the first week
- **Materials** are uploaded and downloaded without relying on a side-channel chat
- **Posts and comments** happen without the feed feeling empty
- **Connections and messages** are used between students who met on Sturum
- Feedback is collected and turned into concrete improvements

Vanity metrics (total signups alone) matter less than **weekly active students in the target department**.

---

## Out of Scope (Current Phase)

To stay focused, the following are explicitly **not** current goals:

- Replacing WhatsApp for all student communication
- Competing with Instagram, TikTok, or LinkedIn at global scale
- Official university accreditation or LMS replacement
- Monetization or ads (until core utility is proven)
- Open cross-department social discovery

These may be revisited only if they serve the department-community vision.

---

## Long-Term Direction

If Sturum proves value in one or more departments, future work may include:

- Stronger mobile experience and push notifications
- Moderation, reporting, and community guidelines at scale
- Better search and organization for materials
- Partnerships with course reps or faculty champions (not replacing official systems)
- Expansion to additional departments or institutions **one wedge at a time**

The long-term goal remains unchanged: **be the trusted department layer for student peer life** — connection, conversation, and shared academic resources in one place.

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **What is Sturum?** | A department-scoped social platform for university students |
| **What problem does it solve?** | Fragmented peer connection, discussion, and materials sharing |
| **How is it different?** | Scoped to department + combined feed, chat, and materials |
| **What does success look like?** | A living community in at least one department, not empty signups |

**Sturum exists so coursemates can find each other, share what helps them pass and grow, and stay in touch — inside the boundary that actually matters: their department.**
